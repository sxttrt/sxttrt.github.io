(2) Regional Comparison of Challenges

Juneau: Overtourism vs. Ecological & Infrastructure Contradictions
1. Scale: 1.6M visitors in 2023 (51x the local population); daily peaks reaching 20k.
2. Ecological Crisis: Mendenhall Glacier retreating by >35km²/year; two major floods in 2023-24 damaging 300+ buildings.
3. Infrastructure: Traffic congestion increased 1.5x; helicopter noise; water/waste systems near collapse.
Hawaii: Imbalanced Distribution & Ecological Degradation
1. Imbalance: 11.34M total visitors in 2023; Oahu took 56% while Kauai took only 14%, leading to simultaneous overload and idle capacity.
2. Ecological Threat: Tourism-related carbon emissions causing sea warming; coral bleaching increased by 40% (2015-19).
3. Local Pressure: Oahu daily visitors >30k; traffic congestion up 28% compared to 2019.

2. Hawaii Model Adaptation

Parameters were adjusted for the four-island scenario, focusing on island-specific carrying capacities:

(1) Parameter Comparison and Environmental Stress

To address Hawaii’s overtourism, the model was refined for four regions including Oahu and Maui County.

Infrastructure pressure formulas were adjusted: due to missing port ranking data, coastal zone indicators were used, resulting in an infrastructure score of 1.5998. The maximum annual visitor volume was set at 6M. Income functions integrated the local tourism tax rate (17.962%), while resident satisfaction was modeled via exponential fitting of unemployment vs. visitor volume across the islands.

Environmental pressure focused on coral bleaching, establishing a four-dimensional dynamic system: “Visitor Volume ($V$) — Carbon Emissions — Sea Temperature Rise — Coral Bleaching Rate ($B$)”. By fitting 2015-2019 data, we derived the final calculation formula for environmental pressure:

$$B = 5.921 \times 10^{-8} \cdot V^3$$

Finally, these four indicators were integrated into a multi-objective model to analyze the relationship between target scores and annual visitor volumes.

IV. Value and Policy Recommendations

1. Three-Tier Policy System for Juneau

Based on optimization results, we proposed a “Short-term Control, Medium-term Balance, Long-term Upgrade” policy framework:

(1) Short-term (1-2 years): Relieving Core Tensions

  • Volume Control: Cap annual visitors at 2.1M (a 31% increase from 2023) and implement a “Cruise-free Saturday.”
  • Infrastructure: Expand waste treatment capacity in downtown by 30% and add dedicated shuttle lanes.
  • Monitoring: Establish real-time “Visitor-Glacier Melt” databases via new sensor nodes.

(2) Medium-term (3-5 years): Balancing Stakeholders

  • Differential Taxation: Implement a $15/person “Ecological Protection Tax” for cruise passengers.
  • Community Synergy: Create “Buffer Zones” between tourist areas and residential zones; utilize tax revenue to improve local water systems.
  • Mechanism: Quarterly “Tourism-Community” hearings where residents hold a 23% veto power over high-impact projects.

(3) Long-term (5+ years): Sustainable Upgrading

  • Diversification: Develop “Glacier Research Tours” and “Indigenous Culture Experiences” to increase average stay from 1.2 to 2.5 days.
  • Green Transition: Mandate biofuel use for cruise ships by 2030 (40% emission reduction).
  • Regional Coordination: Establish regional diversion agreements with Anchorage to prevent localized pressure concentration.
