Customized Design and Manufacturing of Multi-rotor UAVs Based on Large Language Models

Integrating Large Language Models (LLM) with intelligent manufacturing to achieve automated frame generation and customized power module design, covering the full development lifecycle from modeling and manufacturing to flight verification.

2025-06-05
简体中文

I. Project Overview

Category Details
Project Title Customized Design and Manufacturing of Multi-rotor UAVs Based on LLM
Project Lead Chengtao Liu
Timeline April 2025 - June 2025
Core Responsibilities Designed and assembled the power modules (mechanical structure, electronic control, flight control development); Engineered the vision-guided assembly scheme; Conducted flight testing and system tuning.
Core Objectives 1. Automate UAV frame generation using LLMs to enhance design efficiency; 2. Develop customized power modules ensuring high performance and reliability; 3. Achieve synchronized multi-flight controller management and stable flight; 4. Establish an automated pipeline for customized UAV design and manufacturing.
Key Tools SolidWorks (3D Modeling), Bambu Lab 3D Printers (A1/X1), STM32 Open-source Flight Control, Brushless Motors (A2212 series), ESCs (30A), Anonymous Ground Station, Altair Inspire (Topology Optimization), CadQuery (Parametric Design).

II. Background and Objectives

1. Project Background

As demand for personalized and customized UAV applications grows, traditional design and manufacturing processes face challenges such as low efficiency, high costs, and slow response times. Conventional frame design relies heavily on manual modeling, and power module manufacturing depends on manual assembly—processes that are cumbersome, prone to error, and difficult to scale for diverse requirements.

This project innovatively integrates Large Language Models (LLM) with Intelligent Manufacturing: the UAV frame is automatically generated via LLM to significantly boost design efficiency and precision. Given the higher requirements for structural strength and functionality, the power modules utilize a “Manual Design + 3D Printing” approach combined with vision-guided assembly to ensure precise fitment with the frame. This culminates in an automated design-to-manufacturing workflow capable of rapid response to customized needs.

2. Core Objectives

  • Design: Realize LLM-driven automated frame generation and customized power module design, balancing structural compactness with functional integrity.
  • Manufacturing: Utilize 3D printing for frames and power module housings to ensure dimensional accuracy and structural robustness.
  • Control: Implement synchronized communication across multiple flight controllers and validate stable flight based on dual-loop PID algorithms.
  • Workflow: Bridge the gap between “LLM Design → 3D Printing → Electronic Integration → Flight Testing” to verify the feasibility of customized solutions.

III. Key Tasks and Progress

1. Frame Design (LLM-Aided Automated Generation)

Led by team member Peijiang Li (First Author), the frame design employs an LLM-driven automated workflow:

  1. Requirement Analysis: Utilized prompt engineering and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to transform user requirements into structured design goals and initial conditions for topology optimization.
  2. Parametric Design: LLM-generated structural description language was fed into CadQuery to automatically generate base models for quadrotor/hexarotor configurations.
  3. Topology Optimization: Utilized Altair Inspire to optimize critical components like motor arms, achieving lightweighting while maintaining structural strength.
  4. Manufacturing Output: Optimized design files were sent to 3D printers, with frame dimensions controlled at approximately 240mm×240mm to fit the maximum build volume.

2. Power Module Design and Manufacturing

As the module lead, I managed the full lifecycle development from mechanical structure and hardware selection to form-factor optimization:

2.1 Mechanical Design (SolidWorks Modeling)

  • Developed 3D models for the power module housings based on the physical dimensions of motors, ESCs, batteries, and STM32 flight control boards.
  • Optimized for installation space, cable routing, heat dissipation, and structural integrity, with pre-drilled M3 screw holes for stable frame coupling.
  • Focused on utility and maintainability, providing sufficient clearance for debugging and component replacement.

2.2 3D Printing and Manufacturing

  • Equipment: Bambu Lab A1/X1 printers; slicing performed via Bambu Studio.
  • Material: PLA Basic (selected for thermal stability, low shrinkage, and high molding precision, meeting the mechanical needs of the prototype).
  • Efficiency: Printing time per module housing was approx. 2 hours, supporting high-frequency structural iterations.
  • Precision: Layer height set to 0.15mm with dimensional tolerances within ±0.2mm to ensure seamless frame integration.

2.3 Hardware Selection and Optimization

Component Model/Parameters Optimization & Selection Rationale
Motor A2212 1400KV (Replaced 980KV) The 980KV was better suited for larger props; the new 1400KV is optimized for 7-inch props, providing ample thrust and stability at 11V.
Propellers 7-inch 3-blade (7045) Replaced 10-inch props to solve frame clearance issues; 3-blade design increased thrust by 20-30% over 2-blade variants.
ESC 30A Brushless ESC Provides sufficient current overhead for A2212 motors, preventing overload and ensuring reliability.
Flight Controller STM32 Open-source (4cm×4cm) Supports protocol modification and multi-FC synchronization; compact size fits modular design.
Battery 1300mAh 30C 3S/11.1V Compact (72x34x21mm) and lightweight; discharge rate meets high-thrust power requirements.

2.4 Power Module Size and Form-factor Optimization

Addressed issues of excessive volume, high aerodynamic drag, and low space utilization in the initial design:

2.4.1 Optimization Goals
  • Size Optimization: Reduced total volume from 120x80x60mm to 95x70x50mm (~32% reduction).
  • Aero Optimization: Implemented a streamlined curved surface design to minimize drag during flight.
  • Weight Optimization: Removed redundant materials via topology optimization, resulting in an 18% weight reduction while maintaining strength.
2.4.2 Optimization Strategies
  1. Integrated Layout: Redesigned internal placement for the ESC, FC, and battery to eliminate redundant gaps.
  2. Streamlined Surface: Changed the boxy housing to a tapered, aerodynamic structure with rounded edges (R5mm).
  3. Weight Reduction: Used Altair Inspire to hollow out non-load-bearing areas.
  4. Interface Refinement: Adjusted frame-to-module interfaces to match the new dimensions, ensuring assembly precision.

3. Electronic Control System Development

3.1 Multi-FC Synchronous Communication

  • Communication Mechanism: Configured the 2.4GHz receiver to broadcast mode, transmitting control signals to 4 flight controllers simultaneously.
  • Optimization: Disabled return signals and ACK responses from the 3 “slave” controllers to prevent signal collision, retaining only the primary controller’s feedback.
  • Control Logic: Each controller maps synchronized signals into specific motor RPM commands based on its spatial role, achieving collaborative control.

3.2 Dual-loop PID Implementation

  • Architecture: Outer loop (Attitude) controls Roll, Pitch, and Yaw; inner loop (Rate) controls angular velocity.
  • Core Algorithms: Combined Quaternions with Kalman Filtering to fuse MPU6050 data, suppressing noise and drift while resolving Gimbal Lock issues.
  • Parameter Tuning:
    • Initial: Outer loop $K_p=7.0$, $K_i=0$, $K_d=0$; Inner loop $K_p=1.7-3.0$, $K_d=0.08$, $K_i=0$.
    • Refined: Reduced Yaw PD gains to fix oscillation; increased Pitch/Roll $K_p$ and introduced $K_i$ to eliminate steady-state error.

3.3 Sensor Data Processing

  • Coordinate Alignment: Unified X/Y axes through matrix transformations for modules mounted with a 180° rotation.
  • Attitude Estimation: Fused angular velocity and acceleration data from MPU6050 to improve estimation precision and consistency.

4. Flight Testing and Validation

4.1 Testing Objectives

  • Validate take-off capability and attitude response.
  • Test multi-FC synchronization efficacy.
  • Optimize PID parameters to resolve oscillation and drift.
  • Verify performance gains from power module optimization (Endurance, Maneuverability).

4.2 Results

  • Power Performance: A2212 1400KV motors + 7-inch 3-blade props provided ~2828g of total thrust. With a 1300g take-off weight, the Thrust-to-Weight ratio reached 2.18, ensuring stability.
  • Flight Behavior: Successfully achieved the maiden flight, demonstrating responsive attitude adjustment. Post-optimization, oscillations were significantly mitigated.
  • Module Efficiency: Volume reduction (32%) and weight reduction (18%) led to a 12% increase in endurance and an 8% improvement in turning maneuverability.
  • Key Breakthrough: Successfully validated the full “LLM Design → 3D Printing → Electronic Integration → Flight Test” pipeline.

IV. Core Technical Implementation

1. LLM-Aided Frame Design Workflow

The primary innovation lies in converting natural language requirements into automated design outputs:

  1. Initial Input: Receive customized user requirements processed via LLM Prompt Engineering and CoT.
  2. Design Generation: Generate parametric commands into CadQuery for automated UAV frame modeling.
  3. Optimized Manufacturing: Perform topology optimization via Altair Inspire before 3D printing.
  4. Validation Loop:
    • Conduct FEA (Finite Element Analysis) on printed parts. If Passed → Final Output.
    • If Failed → Re-iterate through CoT processing to refine design instructions.

2. Power System Adaptation

Matching power components based on propeller thrust theory: $$T \approx C_{t} \cdot \rho \cdot n^{2} \cdot D^{4}$$

  • Where: $C_t$ is the thrust coefficient, $\rho$ is air density, $n$ is RPM, and $D$ is propeller diameter.
  • Logic: Reversed calculations to find the optimal prop size given frame constraints, resulting in the selection of 7-inch 3-blade props.

3. Structural Optimization Techniques

  • Topology Optimization: Used FEA via Altair Inspire to identify non-critical areas for material reduction.
  • Aerodynamic Design: Streamlined modeling to reduce drag, improving battery life and agility.
  • Integrated Packaging: Compact component placement to minimize redundant volume.

4. Multi-FC Synchronization

  • Communication Architecture: Broadcast mode + Master-Slave roles for synchronized command execution.
  • Protocol Tuning: Disabled slave feedback to eliminate latency and communication conflict.
  • Fault Tolerance: Modular redundancy ensures basic functionality even if a single module fails.

V. Limitations and Future Work

1. Current Limitations

  • Power Consistency: Independent batteries per module lead to slight thrust variances, affecting precision.
  • Sensor Fusion: IMUs operate independently; a lack of cross-module data fusion leads to slight attitude estimation drift.
  • Flight Features: Currently limited to basic attitude control; lacks GPS station-keeping and fail-safe protocols.
  • Engineering Aesthetics: Design remains experimental with lower space efficiency compared to industrial models.

2. Future Directions

  • Power Management: Introduce battery balancing mechanisms to unify thrust across modules.
  • Advanced Fusion: Establish inter-module communication for multi-IMU data fusion to suppress drift.
  • Autonomy Upgrade: Integrate barometers and GPS for altitude-hold and autonomous navigation.
  • Structural Material: Explore carbon fiber composite 3D printing to further reduce weight and increase rigidity.

VII. Conclusion

This project successfully demonstrates a customized UAV development workflow based on LLM and Intelligent Manufacturing. Key outcomes include:

  1. Bridged the “LLM Design → 3D Printing → Control Integration → Flight Test” loop, proving the feasibility of the customized paradigm.
  2. Completed full-stack development of the power modules, achieving significant volume (32%) and weight (18%) reductions alongside synchronized control and stable flight.
  3. Published innovative research in Procedia CIRP, providing a scalable approach for customized multirotor production.

By validating “LLM + Intelligent Manufacturing” through physical prototyping, this project sets a foundation for future iterations involving six-rotor platforms and more complex autonomous mission scenarios.
