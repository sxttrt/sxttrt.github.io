A comprehensive record of reinforcement learning projects, including LQR vs. REINFORCE control for inverted pendulums, and PPO training frameworks for bipedal robots with adaptive walking on complex terrains.

I. Project Core Information

Category Details
Project Title RL-based Inverted Pendulum Control and Bipedal Robot System Construction
Core Team Chengtao Liu, Haojia Zhang, Bohan Ma
Core Objectives 1. Inverted Pendulum: ① Linear control via LQR (Dynamics modeling, linearization, discretization); ② RL control via REINFORCE (Policy gradient deployment, reward shaping);
2. Bipedal Robot: ① Simulation setup in Isaac Gym/MuJoCo; ② PPO training framework deployment; ③ Hierarchical reward function design; ④ Three-stage training pipeline (Balance -> Gait -> Terrain adaptation); ⑤ Adaptive walking on unstructured terrains.
Technical Stack Python, PyTorch (RL Framework), NumPy (Matrix Computing), Isaac Gym (Parallel Simulation), MuJoCo (High-fidelity Physics), Gymnasium (REINFORCE environment), Matplotlib.

II. Background and Objectives

1. Project Background

  • Inverted Pendulum System: As a classic non-linear and unstable system, its balance control is a benchmark for validating control algorithms. Traditional linear control (e.g., LQR) relies on precise mathematical modeling and offers limited robustness. Reinforcement Learning (e.g., REINFORCE) allows agents to learn optimal policies through trial-and-error without an explicit model.
  • Bipedal Robots: These robots offer superior terrain adaptability for search and rescue, exploration, and logistics. However, their high-dimensional, non-linear dynamics make gait stability a core challenge. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) can discover non-intuitive, efficient control policies that outperform traditional model-based methods (e.g., ZMP) in robustness.

2. Core Objectives

  • Inverted Pendulum: Implement LQR-based linear control (covering variable substitution, linearization, and discretization) and REINFORCE-based RL control to achieve long-term balance under micro-perturbations.
  • Bipedal Robot: Deploy a PPO training framework in Isaac Gym; design hierarchical rewards to resolve issues like gait “shuffling” or policy collapse; and establish a three-stage pipeline for adaptive walking on slopes and rough terrain.
  • Engineering Excellence: Modularize all algorithm components and provide simulation visualizations to ensure experiment reproducibility.

III. Key Task Implementation

Task 1: Inverted Pendulum (LQR vs. REINFORCE Control)

1. Linear Control via LQR

(1) Dynamics Modeling, Linearization, and Discretization
  • Variable Substitution: Defined state variables to simplify dynamics: $$x_1=z \text{ (Position)}, x_2=\pi-\theta \text{ (Converted Angle)}, x_3=\dot{z} \text{ (Velocity)}, x_4=\dot{\theta} \text{ (Angular Velocity)}$$
  • Linearization: Performed Taylor expansion at the equilibrium point $x_0=[0,0,0,0]$, assuming $\sin x_2 \approx x_2$ and $\cos x_2 \approx 1$.
  • Discretization: Converted the continuous model to a discrete-time model for LQR design using $A_d = I + AT$ and $B_d = BT$.
(2) LQR Controller Design and Results
  • Parameter Configuration: Defined weight matrices $Q$ and $R$ to balance error and energy consumption. Solved the Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation (DARE) to obtain the optimal feedback gain $K$.
  • Experimental Results: Successfully achieved stability for initial angles of $2.6$, $2.7$, and $2.9\text{ rad}$.

2. RL Control via REINFORCE

  • Strategy: Utilized a two-layer MLP as the policy network, mapping the 4D state to a probability distribution over 2 discrete actions (Left/Right force).
  • Reward Shaping: Designed a multi-dimensional reward function covering survival, angle deviation, centering, and velocity penalties.
  • Results: After 2,325 training episodes, the policy converged, enabling the pendulum to maintain precise balance under continuous perturbations.

Task 2: Bipedal Robot PPO Control

1. System Architecture

  • Isaac Gym: GPU-accelerated simulation supporting thousands of parallel environments. Enabled Domain Randomization (friction, mass, CoM) to enhance policy robustness.
  • MuJoCo: High-fidelity engine used for Sim-to-Sim verification.

2. Hierarchical Reward Function Design

Designed multi-objective rewards to guide behavior:

  • Orientation Reward: Penalized gravity vector projection on the XY plane to keep the torso upright.
  • Z-Velocity Penalty: Suppressed vertical oscillation to stabilize the center of mass.
  • Airtime Reward: Incentivized a target airtime (0.3s - 0.6s) to eliminate “shuffling” and generate natural gaits.
  • Command Tracking: Exponentially penalized errors in linear velocity (XY), angular velocity (Yaw), and heading.
  • Stand-still Reward: Penalized unnecessary motion when command velocity is zero.

3. Three-Stage Training Pipeline

Used a curriculum-based strategy to prevent policy collapse:

  1. Stage 1 (Flat Terrain Balance): Achieved basic standing and mobility. Delayed policy collapse from 5k to 55k episodes by introducing orientation constraints.
  2. Stage 2 (Gait & Command): Optimized gait rhythm and tracking precision by adjusting action rate penalties and airtime rewards.
  3. Stage 3 (Robustness & Complex Terrain): Enabled random force perturbations and terrain curricula (slopes/rough ground), achieving an 88% success rate on non-flat surfaces.

4. Experimental Results

  • Natural Gait: Achieved biological gait characteristics with 0.4s-0.6s airtime and <5% command tracking error.
  • Robustness: Recovered balance within 1.2s-2.0s after random pushes.
  • Terrain Success Rate:

Flat (No Perturbation) 100% < 5°
Unstructured (Trimesh) 90% < 8°
Slopes 92% < 8°
Flat + Random Pushes 95% < 7°

IV. Project Results and Conclusions

1. Simulation Highlights

  • Inverted Pendulum: Demonstrated rapid convergence from extreme initial angles ($2.9\text{ rad}$) using LQR, and robust balance under micro-perturbations using REINFORCE.
  • Bipedal Robot: Generated human-like natural gaits, eliminating “shuffling” and showing high resilience to environmental disturbances and slope changes.

2. Core Technical Insights

  • LQR Weighting: High $Q$ leads to faster response but higher overshoot; High $R$ minimizes energy but slows response.
  • Reward Shaping: Multi-dimensional rewards are critical to prevent agents from exploiting “loopholes” (e.g., surviving without balancing).
  • Curriculum Learning: The three-stage training pipeline is essential for high-dimensional systems; direct training on complex terrains often leads to divergence.
